# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Owners and managers of Brazilian assistance-technical businesses with 1-10 people who receive and
repair phones, computers, electronics, appliances, commercial equipment, or technical tools at a
counter, bench, or workshop. They are often moving between a customer conversation, a device on the
bench, WhatsApp, paper, and parts. Their job on this surface is to recognize a proposed operating
flow, decide whether it addresses a recurring pain, and register qualified interest in a future
pilot without confusing the concept with an available product.

## Product Purpose

Validate whether small repair businesses show recurring pain and willingness to pay for a future
simple system that organizes each item from intake and evidence through diagnosis, quote, approval,
repair/status, delivery, and warranty. Success is trustworthy, qualified pilot/interview interest
and privacy-safe funnel evidence—not SaaS usage, account creation, or revenue.

## Brand Personality

Workbench-practical, instrument-precise, quietly assured. The page should feel like an organized
intake counter just after a chaotic morning has been brought under control: technical enough to be
credible, human enough for a small Brazilian business owner, and direct enough to earn trust.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI startup pages with purple-blue gradients, floating glass cards, empty superlatives, or
  “magic” automation language.
- Gamer, cyberpunk, neon, command-center, or industrial-SCADA imagery.
- Promotional electronics-store layouts dominated by broken-phone stock imagery, price bursts, or
  retail urgency.
- Fake dashboards presented as a live product, invented testimonials, customer logos, metrics, or
  ratings.
- Editorial-magazine styling, serif-italic theater, monospace-as-technical costume, repetitive
  eyebrow labels, endless identical card grids, and card-within-card compositions.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the repair journey, not software theater.** The lifecycle and its evidence are the hero;
   conceptual UI is supporting proof and is always labeled.
2. **Signal validation before persuasion.** The visitor must understand the product is being shaped
   with real workshops before being asked to submit.
3. **Organize visible complexity.** Use sequence, grouping, and calm contrast to make a messy
   workshop process feel controllable without pretending the work is simple.
4. **Earn trust through specificity.** Use real domain nouns—equipment, accessories, IMEI/serial,
   quote, approval, collection, warranty—while avoiding unsupported capability claims.
5. **One experiment, equal craft.** Preserve the shared visual and conversion structure needed for
   fair comparison with the other vertical pages; domain differences live in copy, examples, and
   the conceptual process view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support full keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings/landmarks,
real labels, associated errors and live status, 200% zoom, reduced motion, and high contrast on the
dark theme. Do not rely on color alone for state. Use plain Brazilian Portuguese and preserve
legibility for owners reading quickly on a phone in mixed workshop lighting.
